Firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1

A no-input mixer, with drums

Lilliput turns the FM-1 into a no-input mixer: six channels with nothing plugged into them, feeding back into themselves and into each other, through tubes, op-amps, filters, limiters and a rack of effects. The circuit is the instrument. You play it by finding where a loop starts to sing.

Beside it sits a four-voice drum synthesizer and a rhythm engine on one shared tempo, so the feedback can be played as drones, as texture, or chopped and pushed into rhythmic noise.

What it does

Six channels
Each with an input selector, noise, gain, a choice of tubes and op-amps, a wavefolder, high and low filters, a resonant middle filter, a brickwall limiter whose look-ahead sets the loop's pitch, two sends, level and pan. Every channel can feed itself, the mix bus, the sends or any other channel.
The rack
Seven effect slots: on a channel, on a send loop, or on the output. Delay, flanger, chorus, bitcrusher, resonator, ring and cross modulation, octaver, reed, sag, cuts and more. The output adds an equaliser and SPACE, a reverb with six rooms.
Recipes
HOME draws a whole new patch: new connections, new settings and new motion, from any of twenty-five recipes (DRONE, SIREN, GEIGER, ENGINE, CAVERN, FREE and more). Every patch sounds at once. SHIFT + HOME stays in the recipe on the screen.
Motion
Two LFOs and an eight-slot modulation matrix on every channel, so the patch moves by itself.
Rhythm
A rhythm engine that gates, chokes or strikes the loops in sixteen styles, from four-on-the-floor to Turing machines.
Drums
Four synthesized voices, kick, snare, hats and metallic percussion, each with its own drive, on the rhythm's tempo and beat. Twenty-eight sounds and twelve rhythms written for rhythmic noise and industrial techno.
Performance
A gesture loop on every channel, sixteen snapshots with morphing, and sixty-four sessions in the FM-1's flash, which you can back up to a computer.

Getting it

Lilliput is free software (GPL-3.0). You need an M-VAVE FM-1, a USB cable that carries data, and a desktop Chrome, Edge or Opera: the installer runs in the browser and writes the firmware over USB MIDI. It runs on both of the FM-1's processor cores. The manual covers every control, and the changelog every build.

Lilliput is unofficial: not made, endorsed or supported by M-VAVE. Installing it may void your device's warranty. Its design follows Igor Vasiliev's NoInputMixer app.
